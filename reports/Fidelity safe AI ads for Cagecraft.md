# Lock the garment, generate everything else

The AI creative CAGECRAFT can run without the owner flinching is any technique where the garment pixels stay original photography and AI produces only what sits behind, around or between product shots: AI-generated backgrounds under a locked real cutout, AI b-roll hooks with no garment in frame, code-driven motion graphics over real stills, and AI script, voice, music and captions. Everything that passes the garment through a generative model (virtual try-on, AI models, generated packshots, image-to-video on the product, AI avatars wearing it) fails a strict "no change" test most of the time: in the best published benchmark, the strongest image models reproduced a product with zero flagged differences in **only 29% of generations**, and logo and text distortion was the single most common failure ([Photoroom](https://www.photoroom.com/blog/top-editing-image-models-maintain-product-details-only-28-of-the-time)). That is the worst possible profile for a printed mesh jersey. The constraint costs CAGECRAFT very little, because the ads that have survived longest in the Meta Ad Library (pulled 16 November 2026) are plain product layouts and branded motion templates, not generated imagery. The best ads to recreate are, for static, the **front-and-back garment pair on white** (live 369 days at Shadow Rolls, 321 days for BaddieCat's mesh jersey), the **350gsm hoodie colour grid** (Realism, 273 days) and the **spec-callout hoodie** (Rogue 381 days, Snobodi 82 days); for video, **FUJI's 15-second product-ticker template** (534 days, the longest-running fightwear video found), the **OverRip annotated hoodie explainer** (343 days) and **Raven's colour-swap motion graphic** (140 days). Three caveats frame all of this: "winning" here means longevity and duplication in the Ad Library, not spend or ROAS, which Meta does not publish; the pull landed in Black Friday fortnight, which hides the evergreen ads of sale-heavy accounts such as Engage; and the strongest apparel videos in both competitor sets are real creator clips that AI cannot safely imitate.

## Regenerating the garment fails two times in three

The techniques split on one question: are the garment pixels original photography, or did a model re-synthesise them? Current image editors "synthesize a new output rather than editing the source image directly", so a prompt that says "keep the product" protects nothing ([Photoroom](https://www.photoroom.com/blog/fidelity-gap-ai-product-photography)). Photoroom's July 2026 benchmark put 850 products through four leading models with at least three trained annotators per image and a one-flag-fails rule. **Nano Banana 2 passed 29.0%, Nano Banana Pro 28.2%, GPT Image 2 27.2% and FLUX.2 Klein 16.8%.** The failure list reads like CAGECRAFT's spec sheet: **logo and text distortion in 20.1% of generations**, missing or changed elements 12.5%, pattern or design changes 11.4%, colour shifts 8.1%, wrongly added elements such as a zip or button 4.8%, material or texture changes 3.1% ([Photoroom](https://www.photoroom.com/blog/top-editing-image-models-maintain-product-details-only-28-of-the-time)). A correction layer lifted the best model only to 38.2%, and Photoroom separately flags mesh as especially vulnerable ([Photoroom](https://www.photoroom.com/blog/fidelity-gap-ai-product-photography)). An independent studio test on a single dress found every model changed sleeve length, hem length or neckline, and concluded that where information is missing "the AI invents it" ([Orbitvu](https://orbitvu.com/blog/generative-ai-vs-reality-how-do-virtual-try-ons-compare-real-model-content)).

Video is no safer. The best product image-to-video test available used a skincare bottle, not apparel; Seedance 2.5 and Kling O3 scored 29 of 30 on preservation with the product locked stationary, yet the author still warned that "small typography and packaging elements still need checking" because details "loosen during the move" ([getimg.ai](https://getimg.ai/blog/best-image-to-video-model-for-product-ads-test)). Published guidance for AI video is to "keep critical text out of the generated layer and composite it afterward" ([Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/ai-video-product-swap-ads-ecommerce-local-redraw-2026)). No independent benchmark exists for logo retention on printed garments in video, for mesh, or for heavy fleece, so every claim that a video model "holds" a print is untested for this product. Generative upscalers belong in the same bucket: they alter stitching, label text and weave while looking sharper ([LetsEnhance](https://letsenhance.io/blog/all/ai-upscaling-makes-images-worse/)).

The table below is a synthesis of that evidence into a working rule for CAGECRAFT. The ordering is a judgement; no published source ranks all techniques on one apparel scale.

| Verdict | Technique | What AI touches | Typical failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Run it | AI-generated background or scene, original cutout composited on top as a locked layer | Background only | Lighting or scale mismatch, halo edges. Garment unchanged |
| Run it | AI b-roll or hook footage with no garment in frame, hard cut to real product | Atmosphere only | None to the garment; AI people draw labels and backlash |
| Run it | Code-driven motion design over real stills (crop, scale, pan, masks, type) | Nothing generative | Ordinary cutout or colour-management errors |
| Run it | AI script, voice-over, music, captions | No visuals | Script over-claims |
| Run after a look | Layer parallax between the flat cutout and a separate background | Background edges | Warped print if a depth model works inside the garment |
| Per-asset QC, expect rejects | One-click "AI scene" tools that relight or harmonise the subject; diffusion relighting; generative upscaling | Colour, shading, texture of the garment | Colourway shift, flattened fleece nap or mesh holes |
| Per-asset QC, expect rejects | Image-to-video from a real photo, camera-only move | Every frame after the first | Flicker, logo loosening mid-clip |
| Do not run | Generated packshots or ghost mannequins, reference-conditioned edits, virtual try-on, AI models | The whole garment | Print, proportion and construction drift |
| Do not run | Garment-in-motion image-to-video, AI avatars wearing or holding product, text-to-video | The whole garment, every frame | All of the above plus invented fabric physics and back views |

Two tools already in this workflow sit on opposite sides of that line. A Nano Banana 2 packshot generator re-renders the garment and belongs in the "do not run" rows; Remotion-style coded motion design never synthesises a product pixel.

The safe pattern has a credible precedent at scale. Wayfair's head of performance creative describes "invisible AI": environments generated around real products, with the product required to match the real item's material, texture and scale. An AI room built on real products topped its test round, while fully AI scenes not tied to real products were rejected by customers ([Motion Creative Strategy Summit](https://runneth.com/library/talk/how-to-build-an-ai-advertising-system-that-scales-without-losing-brand-trust/)). That evidence is furniture, not apparel, and no source measures AI-background composites against plain photography for clothing.

The owner's instinct also matches where the reputational and legal risk sits in Australia. Melbourne label Atoir was called "deceptive" on Reddit for AI model imagery on The Iconic even though it was disclosed, with shoppers asking how they could judge a garment that "probably" was not real ([Pedestrian.tv](https://www.pedestrian.tv/tech-gaming/redditors-slam-the-iconic-melbourne-brand-for-ai-models/)), and Jeanswest's AI video spots were ridiculed with "are you pranking us?" ([B&T](https://www.bandt.com.au/are-you-pranking-us-jeanswest-ridiculed-online-over-ai-monstrosity/)). The ACCC requires that the "overall general impression" of images and descriptions is accurate and says intent "makes no difference" ([ACCC](https://www.accc.gov.au/business/advertising-and-promotions/false-or-misleading-claims)). Documented backlash centres on AI people and visible glitches; no case was found where the complaint was a wrong print or seam, so the return-rate consequence of a drifted garment is unmeasured.

## Fightwear's longest-running ads are templates and raw clips

Engage dominates Australian fightwear volume with roughly 270 active ads, but none is older than 40 days because the account was rebuilt for Black Friday; its most duplicated creative is a typography-only "NOW LIVE" poster with no product in it, accounting for 32 of the 53 Engage ads sampled ([Engage](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1506752394850728)). The evergreen winners belong to smaller brands. **FUJI Sports has run the same 15-second product-ticker video and a four-photo collage static since 30 May 2025, 534 days** ([FUJI video](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1435982267851469), [FUJI collage](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1068325335158879)). Shadow Rolls has kept a front-and-back rash guard on pure white live for 369 days and reuses the template on designs live 257 and 154 days ([Shadow Rolls](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1228465485758821)). Australian brand Raven runs only four ads in total, and two of them are colour-swapping motion graphics live since June ([Raven](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1013010124545097)).

The other long-runners are real people. Battlegend's "POV: I found the best rashguard" reveal clip has run 381 days ([Battlegend](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=847939574468902)), and Anaconda's coach testimonial has run 348 days with the highest Meta variant count found, five ads sharing the creative ([Anaconda](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1256238793205290)); a third-party tracker reports 80 of Anaconda's 82 tracked ads are creator partnership ads ([AdManage](https://admanage.ai/ad-library/anaconda-fightwear)). These are a sourcing task, not a design task, and no fidelity-safe AI technique produces them.

Three patterns matter for recreation. None of the long-running statics shows a price, discount badge or star rating; offer treatment appears only in sale creative, where it is the whole ad. Long-running videos open on the product within the first second and cluster at 5 to 20 seconds for brand pieces or 55 to 67 seconds for testimonials. And no ad in the fightwear set carried Meta's AI label, although several look synthetic on inspection: Engage's poster paste-up video reads as a generated or CG scene with the real poster artwork composited in ([Engage video](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1891204938537501)), XMartial's Phantom flat-lays show image-model tells ([XMartial](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1723269489422350)), and the Shadow Rolls garments are 3D mock-ups, not photographs. Those calls are visual judgements only. The Engage video is the instructive one: it is AI used exactly the fidelity-safe way, with no garment generated. CAGECRAFT's own 18 visible ads already use several of the right hooks ("Diamond or Cotton Candy?", "The Walkout Hoodie | 3 Colourways", "Claim Your Free Kit Bag") ([Cagecraft](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=2306553086866799)), and in the partly rate-limited Australian searches no other fightwear advertiser used "fight jersey" or "walkout" as product terms, a weak signal that the positioning is uncontested in paid social.

## Adjacent hoodie and jersey winners sell weight, colour and construction

Gym and streetwear brands give the closest product matches. Realism's "Most Wanted" static, a 4×2 grid of hoodie cutouts on off-white with "350gsm fleece" in the caption and no price, has run 273 days under two ad IDs ([Realism](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=890569587284445)). Every hoodie static catalogued states fabric weight as a number: 350gsm at Snobodi, 410gsm at Ryderwear, 450GSM at Kaizen. Snobodi shows the two proven ways to present construction, thin arrows from short labels to the garment ("350GSM Fleece — Thick. Toasty. Cozy") and a ruled spec panel beside a fabric macro ([Snobodi](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1024491830587105)), and Rogue's hero-plus-two-print-macros collage has run 381 days ([Rogue](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=778045878611503)). For printed mesh jerseys the winners are plainer still: BaddieCat's front-and-back cutout pair on white has run 321 days across Australia and the US with the headline "This is the jersey people will ask about" ([BaddieCat](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1274590234454319)), and Tokyo-Tiger pairs a bare cutout with a customer-voice quote ([Tokyo-Tiger](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1409106991111411)).

On video, **20 of 21 long-running adjacent videos show a person wearing the product**, and the leaders by longevity are creator try-ons such as Ryderwear's Sculpt clip at 477 days ([Ryderwear](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=755338930312127)). Two exceptions carry the argument instead of the body. OverRip's 48-second hoodie explainer, live 343 days, is a presenter in a small rectangle over still garment photos with red hand-drawn annotations on shoulder seams and waist taper ([OverRip](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=2986359731557170)). Thank God's 25-second "buy one hoodie, get two tees" explainer, live 199 days in the US and 153 in Australia with four ads sharing the creative on each of two US ad IDs, proves the offer with a screen recording of the cart ([Thank God](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1511388817161044)). The cheapest pattern that still clears 100 days is a designed product card held over minimal footage, as in Gymreapers' DNA Tee and Hilyfe's tick-list overlay ([Gymreapers](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1624400265913744), [Hilyfe](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=819329707893869)).

The aggregate data agrees with the library, with one warning. Motion's Creative Benchmarks 2026, covering $1.29B of Meta spend across 578,750 creatives ([Motion](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/)), ranks "Text only" and "Product image with text" first and second of 15 asset types on both hit rate and spend efficiency, while "Animation" ranks 14th ([Motion](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-asset-types)). In Fashion and Apparel specifically, the top formats by hit rate are Post-it, Quiz, Stylized product shot, Meme, ASMR, Product shot and Social comment, and Text message and Slideshow rank fourth and seventh by spend efficiency ([Motion](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/visual-formats-by-vertical)). Motion defines a winner by spend, its window includes Black Friday, and it publishes rank order only for apparel. The reading that reconciles "animation under-performs" with FUJI's 534-day template is that CAGECRAFT's coded video should look like product-forward templates, slideshows and annotated stills, not polished motion-graphics explainers.

The adjacent set also shows what crossing the line looks like. On visual inspection, Kaizen's 160-day colour infographic uses rendered hoodies with identical folds across three colours ([Kaizen](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1534274128234078)), and Nofltrs runs a near-static "video" in which the hoodie and its back print read as generated ([Nofltrs](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1360706332318370)). Neither identification is confirmed by the advertiser or by metadata. Both layouts are worth borrowing; both garments are exactly what CAGECRAFT's owner would reject.

## A truthful asset can still be altered after upload

The build discipline is short. Keep the original cutout on its own layer; generate backgrounds with no garment in the prompt; match light direction and camera height at the prompt stage and grade the background toward the product, never the reverse; add shadows as a separate layer; and compare the export against the source inside the product area ([Dreamina](https://dreamina.capcut.com/ai-image/change-product-background-preserve-product)). Features named Harmonize, relight or staging re-render the subject and stay out of the pipeline. Reference photos fed to any generator must be clean, because one developer test saw measurement text on a source photo rendered onto the shirt as part of the design ([Suzuki Shoten](https://suzuki-shoten.pages.dev/blog/metafit-genai-part2)). Any asset from the amber rows gets the benchmark-grade check: zoomed side-by-side against the source by someone who knows the physical garment, one flag fails ([Photoroom](https://www.photoroom.com/blog/top-editing-image-models-maintain-product-details-only-28-of-the-time)).

The largest residual risk sits with Meta, not the brand's own tools. Advantage+ creative enhancements have been on by default since October 2025 and can generate up to 150 variations, and **from 30 October 2026 Meta's terms make advertisers "solely responsible" for what its AI features change** ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/meta-makes-advertisers-solely-responsible-for-its-ai-ad-edits-from-oct-30/)). Tights brand Snag found about 5% of its ads altered, including changed bodies and a three-legged model, after opting out of every feature ([Glossy](https://www.glossy.co/fashion/meta-is-auto-generating-ai-ads-for-its-advertisers-causing-headaches-for-image-conscious-fashion-brands/)), and REI's real bike photo ran for about a week with extra handlebars; reporting found no Meta feature that locks visual elements against modification ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/reis-ai-bike-ad-fiasco-reveals-a-meta-auto-enrollment-setting-brands-missed/)). Every ad needs each enhancement unchecked, rechecked on duplication, and the delivered preview inspected.

Labelling is a smaller matter. Meta applies "AI info" to ads made with its own generative tools and auto-detects third-party AI through industry-standard signals; the label sits behind the three-dot menu unless a photorealistic AI human is present, when it moves beside "Sponsored" ([Meta](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/02/gen-ai-transparency-metas-ads-products/)). TikTok lists its AI-generated content disclaimer as mandatory for AI-generated or significantly AI-modified media ([TikTok](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/about-ad-disclaimers-in-tiktok-ads-manager?lang=en)), which on a plain reading covers an AI background or AI hook even when the garment is untouched. Keeping AI people out of every asset keeps the Meta label in its least prominent position and avoids the lightning rod in every documented backlash.

Finally, pace and variety. Accounts spending under US$10K a month test about 2.8 creatives a week at a 4.0% hit rate ([Motion](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/testing-volume-by-tier)), and one agency reports Meta's retrieval system groups visually similar ads into a single entity, so ten headline variants on one hoodie photo do not get ten chances ([Common Thread Collective](https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/meta-andromeda-roas-creative-strategy-2026)). With fixed product photography, structural variety across the shortlist rows is the lever. Each concept ships as a 4:5 master (1080×1350) for feed and a 9:16 master (1080×1920) for Reels, Stories and TikTok ([Ads Uploader](https://adsuploader.com/blog/meta-ads-aspect-ratios)).

## Conclusion

The research inverts the usual framing of AI ad production. For CAGECRAFT the scarce input is not generation capacity but photography: a clean front and back cutout, a flat-lay and two macros per colourway, plus a few minutes of phone footage, determine how much of this shortlist is buildable, and AI's job is the cheap scaffolding around those pixels. The competitors using AI most visibly are the ones generating garments (Kaizen, Nofltrs) or flooding templated statics (RUNNR, still advertising a "4th of July" price in Australia in November ([RUNNR](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1084354137510814))), which leaves "every garment you see is the one you get" available as a positioning line in a category where shoppers buy the print; Aerie's no-AI-people pledge became its most popular Instagram post ([Marketing Brew](https://www.marketingbrew.com/stories/2026/03/30/aerie-doubles-down-on-its-no-ai-pledge-in-its-latest-campaign)).

This is a test plan, not a proven playbook. No independent case study shows a small apparel brand scaling on this hybrid, no source measures AI-background composites against plain product shots for clothing, and nothing found covers fightwear or Australian benchmarks specifically. The shortlist's top rows carry the strongest longevity evidence and zero depiction risk, so they are the place to spend the first month's three slots a week; the amber techniques earn a trial only after those have a baseline to beat.
