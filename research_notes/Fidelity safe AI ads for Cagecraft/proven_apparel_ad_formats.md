# Proven paid-social ad formats for DTC apparel (Meta + TikTok), 2026 — format catalogue, evidence, and unaltered-product-photo build recipes

Research date: 16 November 2026. Scope: format catalogue and performance evidence only (competitor ad teardown and AI-tooling risk are covered by other researchers).

**Reading conventions used throughout**

- **[DATA]** = quantified dataset or platform-published figure. **[PRACT]** = practitioner or vendor opinion / anecdote. **[SNIPPET]** = seen only in a search-result summary, page not opened; treat as unverified.
- In the format cards, the lines "Looks like", "Copy pattern", "Mistakes" and "Build recipe" are **researcher synthesis** (design recommendations, not sourced facts) unless a link follows. Every performance claim carries a link.
- The single most important dataset is Motion's *Creative Benchmarks 2026*. Note: `motionapp.com/library/...` now 301-redirects to `runneth.com/library/...`, and the pages carry "Runneth" branding with Motion listed as the company; I refer to it as "Motion CB26". It covers $1.29B Meta spend, 578,750 creatives, 6,015 accounts, 1 Sep 2025 – 1 Jan 2026 — [Motion CB26 overview](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/). Three limits apply to every Motion number below: (a) a "winner" is defined by **spend** (≥10x the account's median creative spend and ≥$500), not ROAS or revenue; (b) the window includes BFCM, so promotional formats and hooks are over-represented; (c) the per-vertical pages publish **rank order only**, without numbers — [Motion CB26 overview](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/), [Motion CB26 hook tactics](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-hook-tactics), [Motion CB26 formats by vertical](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/visual-formats-by-vertical).
- "Hit rate" = share of a format's creatives that become winners. "Spend use ratio" = format's share of spend ÷ its share of creative volume; above 1.0 means it earns more spend than its volume share — [Motion CB26 top visual formats](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-visual-formats).

## Key Question 1 — Which static formats are consistently reported as top performers for apparel in 2025-2026, and what copy patterns and visual rules make each work?

### Takeaway

For Fashion & Apparel specifically, the best available dataset (Motion CB26, Sep 2025–Jan 2026) ranks **Post-it, Quiz, Stylized product shot, Meme, ASMR, Product shot, Social comment, Podcast, Product showcase and Unconventional text placement** as the top formats by hit rate — an "aesthetic, discovery and cultural-fluency" set that has almost no overlap with the testimonial / us-vs-them / before-after formats that dominate supplement and skincare advice. Across all verticals, the plainest asset types win most often: **"Text only" and "Product image with text" rank first and second on both hit rate and spend efficiency**, which is exactly what a brand with flat-lay photos can build without touching the garment.

### Cited Findings

**A. Cross-format evidence that frames the whole static catalogue**

- [DATA] Fashion & Apparel top 10 visual formats **by hit rate** (rank order, no values published): 1 Post-it, 2 Quiz, 3 Stylized product shot, 4 Meme, 5 ASMR, 6 Product shot, 7 Social comment, 8 Podcast, 9 Product showcase, 10 Unconventional text placement — [Motion CB26 formats by vertical](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/visual-formats-by-vertical)
- [DATA] Fashion & Apparel top 10 **by spend use ratio** (rank order): 1 Podcast, 2 Unconventional text placement, 3 Billboard, 4 Text message, 5 Sign, 6 Celebrity, 7 Slideshow, 8 Post-it, 9 Offer-first banner, 10 Demo — [Motion CB26 formats by vertical](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/visual-formats-by-vertical)
- [DATA] By contrast, Health & Wellness leaders by hit rate are Stitch, Reaction video, Unboxing, Celebrity, Founder, Letter, Stop motion, Influencer endorsement, POV, Transformation; Motion states top formats "shift substantially" across verticals and there is no universal best — [Motion CB26 formats by vertical](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/visual-formats-by-vertical)
- [DATA] All-vertical leaderboard (only formats used by ≥50 accounts): Offer-first banner 8.6% hit rate, 21.9% of creatives, 29.3% of spend, spend use 1.3; Demo 8.1% / 12.6% / 12.9% / 1.0; Testimonial 6.5% / 13.3% / 13.3% / 1.0; Unboxing 9.8% / 2.1% / 2.8% / 1.3; Celebrity 5.9% / 0.8% / 1.8% / 2.1 — [Motion CB26 top visual formats](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-visual-formats)
- [DATA] Top spend-use formats across all verticals: Celebrity 2.17, Letter 1.87, Unconventional text placement 1.44, Post-it 1.38, Offer-first banner 1.34, Unboxing 1.32; the top four by spend use did not make the top 25 by hit rate — [Motion talk, recorded Apr 2026](https://runneth.com/library/talk/we-analyzed-500k-in-meta-ads-to-find-hooks-formats-that-always-win/)
- [DATA] Asset-type ranking by hit rate (range ~4–12%, individual values not published): Text only; Product image with text; Lifestyle-product image; UGC; High production; GIF; Illustration; UGC mashup; Lifestyle-product image with text; Lifestyle image with text; Lifestyle image; Hybrid; Product image; Animation; Carousel. By spend use (range ~0.5–1.9): Text only; Product image with text; Illustration; UGC; … Animation and Carousel last and "under-capture spend relative to their volume share" — [Motion CB26 top asset types](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-asset-types)
- [DATA] The same Motion team states that text-only ads, product images with text overlays and GIFs produce more winners than high-production ads, illustrating it with a high-production Ridge Wallet ad ("A WALLET AS TOUGH AS YOU ARE") that lost to a low-effort text ad ("Click below to buy our course") — [Motion talk, Apr 2026](https://runneth.com/library/talk/we-analyzed-500k-in-meta-ads-to-find-hooks-formats-that-always-win/)
- [PRACT] Alex Cooper (AdCrate, 16 Jun 2026; oversees 50+ accounts, mostly non-apparel examples) says most winning statics fall into four families: comparison (us vs them, before vs after), **product image statics** (product + headline + benefits — "produced more winners than any other format"), organic statics (press hits, post-it notes, native ads, comment responses), and offer-based ads (less incremental, capture existing demand) — [Alex Cooper newsletter](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/p/how-to-make-better-static-ads-in-2026)
- [PRACT] Cooper's layout rules: statics are scanned in a fraction of a second; control size and placement to guide the eye in order (headline → qualifier → product → review count → primary text); use highlights, arrows, bold, underline; "clear beats clever every time"; mine customer reviews for headlines — [Alex Cooper newsletter](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/p/how-to-make-better-static-ads-in-2026)
- [PRACT] A swipe file of 25 live apparel ads (published 30 Aug 2026, refreshed 14 Nov 2026; no spend/ROAS data, only "active and clears Meta's low-impression flag") is dominated by carousels and single images with one short all-caps benefit line over a tight product crop — [Top Growth Marketing swipe file](https://topgrowthmarketing.com/paid-ads-library/fashion-apparel-ads-that-are-actually-working-right-now/)
- [SNIPPET] GoMarble's library of 250 Gymshark Meta ads (29 Jan–28 Feb 2026) was 78% image and 22% video — [GoMarble Gymshark](https://www.gomarble.ai/library/brands/gymshark/)
- [SNIPPET] Motion's brand page for Gym Glamour (gym apparel) shows 273 active ads, ~14 new creatives a week, leaning on Demo 19%, Headline 15%, Try-on 12% — [Motion Gym Glamour](https://motionapp.com/library/gym-glamour)

**B. Static format cards (S1–S24)**

Canvas assumed for layout notes: 4:5 feed (1080×1350) with a 9:16 variant; see Key Question 4 for safe zones.

**S1. Feature-callout / "anatomy of" diagram**
- Looks like: product cutout centred at 55–65% of frame height on a flat or softly textured background; 3–5 thin leader lines or arrows from specific garment points to 2–5-word labels; headline band in the top 15–20%; logo small in a corner. Motion's library names this "Feature Benefit Pointout — callouts highlighting features and benefits" — [Motion format library](https://runneth.com/library/formats/)
- Copy pattern: headline = one outcome ("Built for the walkout"); labels = spec + consequence ("350gsm fleece — holds shape").
- Angle / funnel: tangible proof of quality and justification of price; cold and consideration.
- Evidence: belongs to "Product image with text", ranked #2 of 15 asset types on both hit rate and spend use [DATA] — [Motion CB26 top asset types](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-asset-types). Cooper calls product + headline + benefits the family with the most winners [PRACT] — [Alex Cooper](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/p/how-to-make-better-static-ads-in-2026). Apparel example: Chubbies split panel — shorts flat on white beside a fabric macro, "OFFICIALLY LICENSED PRODUCT / THE ULTIMATE FAN UNIFORM" — [Top Growth Marketing](https://topgrowthmarketing.com/paid-ads-library/fashion-apparel-ads-that-are-actually-working-right-now/). No apparel-specific hit rate published for this exact format.
- Mistakes: more than 5 callouts; tiny text ("complex explanations in one image fail, creating tiny text and confusion" — [TrendTrack, snippet](https://www.trendtrack.io/blog-post/static-ads-examples)); labels that state a spec without a benefit.
- Real stills + code only: **Yes.** Recipe: background-remove the flat-lay (mask only, pixels untouched), place on a code-drawn canvas, draw SVG leader lines and text. Fabric macro inset must be a real crop of the real photo.

**S2. Us-vs-them comparison table**
- Looks like: two columns (brand vs "typical $60 hoodie" or generic category), 4–6 rows with ticks/crosses, product images on top of each column, headline above; or split-screen. Segwise describes "split screens… unit-price comparisons, and side-by-side quality shots" — [Segwise, 23 Mar 2026](https://segwise.ai/blog/100m-meta-ads-5-formats-dtc)
- Copy pattern: rows the buyer already cares about (weight in gsm, print method, shrinkage, price per wear); "educational, not combative" — [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/100m-meta-ads-5-formats-dtc)
- Angle / funnel: frames the comparison before the shopper does; warm / consideration per Segwise's funnel-matching — [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/100m-meta-ads-5-formats-dtc)
- Evidence: [PRACT] media buyer Curtis Howland (>$100M managed Meta spend) reports static comparison ads drive 60–70% of conversions in many of his accounts; named best fits are supplements and skincare — **not apparel** — [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/100m-meta-ads-5-formats-dtc). Not in Motion's Fashion & Apparel top 10 on either metric — [Motion CB26 formats by vertical](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/visual-formats-by-vertical). (Pre-2025, possibly outdated: a Feb 2024 Motion webinar advised picking the "them" from post-purchase survey answers — [Motion webinar, snippet](https://motionapp.com/library/talk/2024-ad-creative-secrets-from-8-figure-dtc-brands-feat-obvi/).)
- Mistakes: naming a competitor with unprovable claims; using a fake or AI-made "competitor" garment photo; more than 6 rows.
- Real stills + code only: **Yes** (use a generic silhouette icon, not a photo, for "them").

**S3. Review / testimonial card and stacked 5-star reviews**
- Looks like: (a) headline-review: a 3–8-word customer quote as the giant headline, stars, product hero below, review count; (b) stacked: 3–4 review cards offset over a product shot. Motion defines "Review — highlight of a customer review" — [Motion format library](https://runneth.com/library/formats/)
- Copy pattern: cut the most dramatic real review to 3–4 words and put function in the sub-line (example "Saved my marriage" for earplugs) — [Brick by Brick, 12 Dec 2025](https://brick-by-brick.beehiiv.com/p/these-6-statics-are-printing-money-right-now-steal-them); specific results beat generic praise — [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/100m-meta-ads-5-formats-dtc)
- Angle / funnel: social proof; cold through retargeting.
- Evidence: [DATA] Testimonial (all verticals, static + video) 6.5% hit rate, 13.3% of creatives, 13.3% of spend, spend use 1.0 — [Motion CB26 top visual formats](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-visual-formats). Not in the Fashion & Apparel top 10 — [Motion CB26 formats by vertical](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/visual-formats-by-vertical). [PRACT] Cooper cites a winning ad that showed 3,600 reviews as proof (non-apparel) — [Alex Cooper](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/p/how-to-make-better-static-ads-in-2026)
- Mistakes: invented or paraphrased reviews (native look "does not permit fake testimonials" under Meta ad standards / FTC guidance — [adlibrary.com guide, 9 Aug 2026](https://adlibrary.com/guides/native-image-ads-guide)); generic praise; too many cards to read.
- Real stills + code only: **Yes**, provided the review text is real and verbatim.

**S4. Press-quote / PR screenshot card**
- Looks like: publication masthead or article screenshot, a "Best for …" label or pull-quote in large type, product image beneath — [Brick by Brick](https://brick-by-brick.beehiiv.com/p/these-6-statics-are-printing-money-right-now-steal-them)
- Copy pattern: lead with the use-case wording ("Best for …"), not just "as seen in" — [Brick by Brick](https://brick-by-brick.beehiiv.com/p/these-6-statics-are-printing-money-right-now-steal-them)
- Angle / funnel: borrowed authority; cold and sceptical audiences — [Brick by Brick](https://brick-by-brick.beehiiv.com/p/these-6-statics-are-printing-money-right-now-steal-them)
- Evidence: [PRACT] listed by Cooper among "organic statics" — [Alex Cooper](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/p/how-to-make-better-static-ads-in-2026); example is a food brand, **not apparel**, and no performance number is given — [Brick by Brick](https://brick-by-brick.beehiiv.com/p/these-6-statics-are-printing-money-right-now-steal-them)
- Mistakes: implying coverage that does not exist. For a small brand without press, a real gym/fighter/promotion endorsement is the honest substitute.
- Real stills + code only: **Yes if real press exists; otherwise No (not applicable).**

**S5. Post-it / sticky note / handwritten "ugly ad"**
- Looks like: a real product photo (flat-lay or on-model) with a yellow sticky note or hand-written line overlaid, slightly rotated, marker-style font; Motion defines "Post It — sticky-note overlay", plus sibling formats Hand-Drawn, Whiteboard, Chalkboard, Journal Entry — [Motion format library](https://runneth.com/library/formats/)
- Copy pattern: one first-person line, 6–14 words, conversational ("ok this is the heaviest hoodie I own").
- Angle / funnel: pattern interrupt, reads as a note from a person rather than a brand; cold.
- Evidence: [DATA] **#1 format by hit rate in Fashion & Apparel** and #8 by spend use — [Motion CB26 formats by vertical](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/visual-formats-by-vertical); spend use 1.38 across all verticals — [Motion talk](https://runneth.com/library/talk/we-analyzed-500k-in-meta-ads-to-find-hooks-formats-that-always-win/). [PRACT] Cooper lists post-it notes among winning organic statics — [Alex Cooper](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/p/how-to-make-better-static-ads-in-2026)
- Mistakes: polished typography that defeats the purpose; long copy; covering the print or logo with the note.
- Real stills + code only: **Yes** — the easiest format in the catalogue.

**S6. Apple Notes / notes-app screenshot**
- Looks like: an iOS Notes screen — title line, 3–7 short bullet lines, optional inline product photo. Motion: "Notes App — text styled like Apple Notes", catalogued as static-only — [Motion format library](https://runneth.com/library/formats/), [Motion notes-app page, snippet](https://motionapp.com/library/formats/notes-app)
- Copy pattern: list title ("things I wear to fight night"), with the product as one line.
- Angle / funnel: native familiarity; cold.
- Evidence: [SNIPPET] top users in Motion's dataset are app brands (Reframe, Headway, Calm) — **not apparel** — [Motion notes-app page](https://motionapp.com/library/formats/notes-app); not in the Fashion & Apparel top 10 — [Motion CB26 formats by vertical](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/visual-formats-by-vertical). [PRACT, SNIPPET] Creative OS newsletter warns the format "works until it stops working" and that trying visibly hard to look native backfires — [Creative OS #123](https://creativeos.beehiiv.com/p/123-when-native-tries-too-hard)
- Mistakes: wrong UI details; walls of text; no product visible.
- Real stills + code only: **Yes** (HTML/CSS render).

**S7. Text-message / iMessage thread**
- Looks like: 3–5 chat bubbles, last bubble a product photo or link preview. Motion: "Text Message — SMS- or chat-style mockup" — [Motion format library](https://runneth.com/library/formats/)
- Copy pattern: friend-to-friend exchange ending on a question or a reveal ("where's that hoodie from??").
- Angle / funnel: eavesdropping on a private recommendation; cold.
- Evidence: [DATA] **#4 by spend use ratio in Fashion & Apparel** — [Motion CB26 formats by vertical](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/visual-formats-by-vertical). No hit-rate value published.
- Mistakes: scripted, salesy dialogue; presenting a fabricated conversation as a real customer (see S3 caution — [adlibrary.com guide](https://adlibrary.com/guides/native-image-ads-guide)).
- Real stills + code only: **Yes.**

**S8. Social comment / comment-reply and social-post mockup (tweet/X, Reddit, TikTok comment bubble)**
- Looks like: a comment bubble or post card laid over the product photo; Motion defines "Social Comments — screenshot of a comment thread", "Comment Response", and "Social Media Mockup — post mocked up in a platform interface such as Instagram, Twitter, or TikTok" — [Motion format library](https://runneth.com/library/formats/)
- Copy pattern: a real question or objection ("is it actually heavy or just thick?") answered by the image and one line.
- Angle / funnel: objection handling plus social proof; cold and warm.
- Evidence: [DATA] Social comment is **#7 by hit rate in Fashion & Apparel**; Social post mockup is #1 by spend use in Health & Wellness (other category) — [Motion CB26 formats by vertical](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/visual-formats-by-vertical). [PRACT] "comment responses" listed among winning organic statics — [Alex Cooper](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/p/how-to-make-better-static-ads-in-2026)
- Mistakes: invented comments attributed to real-looking handles; platform logos used in a way that implies endorsement.
- Real stills + code only: **Yes**, using real comments.

**S9. Other native UI mimics — Google search bar, ChatGPT-style answer, calendar / reminder notification, AirDrop, alarm**
- Looks like: a single recognisable UI element (search field with a typed query; an AI-chat answer card recommending the product; a lock-screen notification "Drop goes live 6pm") over or beside the product. Motion's taxonomy includes Native Search, ChatGPT, Calendar App Mockup, Alarm App Mockup, AirDrop Mockup, Weather App Mockup, Email Mockup, Checkout Mockup — [Motion format library](https://runneth.com/library/formats/)
- Copy pattern: the query the buyer would actually type ("heavyweight hoodie that doesn't shrink"); notification = time-bound event.
- Angle / funnel: familiarity and curiosity; notification variants suit launches and retargeting.
- Evidence: these exist as catalogued formats but I found **no performance data** for any of them in apparel; none appears in Motion's Fashion & Apparel top 10 — [Motion CB26 formats by vertical](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/visual-formats-by-vertical). [SNIPPET, unverified] one blog attributes to Sprout Social that novelty formats lose CTR faster past frequency 3–4 than evergreen formats — [Influencers Time](https://www.influencers-time.com/fake-text-ads-why-imessage-mockups-beat-studio-video/)
- Mistakes: ChatGPT-style mockups that fabricate a third-party recommendation; treating the gimmick as the idea.
- Real stills + code only: **Yes.**

**S10. Bold-claim headline with product hero**
- Looks like: one all-caps line occupying 25–35% of the frame, tight product crop filling the rest, optional small sub-line; Motion calls the pure version "Headline" — [Motion format library](https://runneth.com/library/formats/)
- Copy pattern (real apparel lines): "THIS HOODIE IS SOFTER THAN A BLANKET" (Mack Weldon), "NEVER IRON A SHIRT AGAIN" and "THE BEST WRINKLE-FREE SHIRT THAT KEEPS SELLING OUT" (UNTUCKit), "A PUFFER FOR YOUR LEGS" (Cotopaxi), "Actually Waterproof" (Vessi) — [Top Growth Marketing](https://topgrowthmarketing.com/paid-ads-library/fashion-apparel-ads-that-are-actually-working-right-now/)
- Angle / funnel: single-benefit clarity; cold. Admetrics calls this "the one core idea", ideally ten words or fewer — [Admetrics, 16 Apr 2026](https://www.admetrics.io/post/five-static-ad-formats-outperforming-video-in-2026)
- Evidence: [DATA] "Product image with text" is #2 asset type on hit rate and spend use — [Motion CB26 top asset types](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-asset-types). Caveat: as a **hook tactic**, "Bold claim" has a 7.19% hit rate, below offer/newness hooks (9–11%) — [Motion talk](https://runneth.com/library/talk/we-analyzed-500k-in-meta-ads-to-find-hooks-formats-that-always-win/)
- Mistakes: clever-but-vague lines; two competing messages; "pretty design, weak headline" — [Alex Cooper](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/p/how-to-make-better-static-ads-in-2026)
- Real stills + code only: **Yes.**

**S11. Statistic / number-led**
- Looks like: one large numeral (30–40% of frame height) with a one-line qualifier and product beside or below; Motion: "Statistic" — [Motion format library](https://runneth.com/library/formats/)
- Copy pattern: a checkable number replaces an adjective — "Every style in sizes 00-40" (Universal Standard), "absorbs up to 9 super tampons worth" (Knix) — [Top Growth Marketing](https://topgrowthmarketing.com/paid-ads-library/fashion-apparel-ads-that-are-actually-working-right-now/); "Trusted by 500,000+ customers" (MoonBrew, non-apparel) — [Brick by Brick](https://brick-by-brick.beehiiv.com/p/these-6-statics-are-printing-money-right-now-steal-them)
- Angle / funnel: specificity as proof; cold and consideration.
- Evidence: examples only; no format-level performance number found.
- Mistakes: numbers that cannot be substantiated; numbers without a unit or consequence.
- Real stills + code only: **Yes** (for this brand: "350gsm").

**S12. Offer-first banner / bundle tiers / gift-with-purchase (and "cart screenshot" variant)**
- Looks like: a discount or bundle statement as the dominant element (top or diagonal banner), product stack or bundle line-up, short CTA; tiered version shows 2–3 columns ("Buy 1 / Buy 2 save 15% / Fight-night bundle"). Cart-screenshot variant recreates a checkout view with line items, crossed-out prices, guarantee — [Brick by Brick](https://brick-by-brick.beehiiv.com/p/these-6-statics-are-printing-money-right-now-steal-them)
- Copy pattern: event label + big number + frictionless CTA — "BLACK FRIDAY – Up To 70% Off / Shop Deals" (True Classic) — [Brick by Brick](https://brick-by-brick.beehiiv.com/p/these-6-statics-are-printing-money-right-now-steal-them); "SAVE $300 WITH THE 12-PIECE SET" (Caraway, non-apparel) — [Motion talk](https://runneth.com/library/talk/we-analyzed-500k-in-meta-ads-to-find-hooks-formats-that-always-win/); price-floor headline "From $24" (Gymshark Women) — [Top Growth Marketing](https://topgrowthmarketing.com/paid-ads-library/fashion-apparel-ads-that-are-actually-working-right-now/)
- Angle / funnel: value and urgency; warm, retargeting, promo periods.
- Evidence: [DATA] Offer-first banner is the highest-volume winning format across verticals (8.6% hit rate; 21.9% of creatives; 29.3% of spend; spend use 1.3) and #9 by spend use in Fashion & Apparel — [Motion CB26 top visual formats](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-visual-formats), [Motion CB26 formats by vertical](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/visual-formats-by-vertical). Offer hooks lead hit rates (Sale announcement 11.35%, Offer only 9.29%) and Giveaway has the top spend use (2.29) — [Motion talk](https://runneth.com/library/talk/we-analyzed-500k-in-meta-ads-to-find-hooks-formats-that-always-win/). Caveats: BFCM window inflates these — [Motion CB26 hook tactics](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-hook-tactics); [PRACT] offer ads are "less incremental" — [Alex Cooper](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/p/how-to-make-better-static-ads-in-2026); vendor data on Indian fashion shows static-discount creative at 1–2.5x ROAS with "very high" saturation — [QuickAds, Mar 2026](https://www.quickads.ai/d2c-fashion-creative-intelligence)
- Mistakes: cluttered multi-offer layouts; discounting a premium positioning constantly.
- Real stills + code only: **Yes.**

**S13. Colourway line-up / collection grid**
- Looks like: 3–6 identical product shots in a row or 2×3 grid, one per colourway, on a uniform background, with a short collection label; or a carousel with one colourway per card.
- Copy pattern: "Limited edition travel collection" over four colourways (Vessi); six tees on hangers with "Best-Selling Gifts For Him" (Vuori); Gymshark carousel "sells colour range" with face cropped out — [Top Growth Marketing](https://topgrowthmarketing.com/paid-ads-library/fashion-apparel-ads-that-are-actually-working-right-now/)
- Angle / funnel: choice and discovery; cold through retargeting.
- Evidence: [DATA] "Product showcase" is #9 by hit rate in Fashion & Apparel — [Motion CB26 formats by vertical](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/visual-formats-by-vertical). Caveat: "Carousel" as an asset type ranks last of 15 on both hit rate and spend use across all verticals — [Motion CB26 top asset types](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-asset-types) — so the single-image grid is the safer build on Meta. On TikTok, the platform recommends 3 or 7–9 images per carousel — [TikTok Image Ads Playbook, undated, likely 2024](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/Image_Ads_Carousel_Ads_Playbook.pdf)
- Mistakes: inconsistent crops/lighting between colourways; recolouring one photo with AI to fake other colourways (a fidelity violation).
- Real stills + code only: **Yes**, if a real photo of each colourway exists.

**S14. Product-on-AI-scene hero ("stylized product shot")**
- Looks like: the real product cutout as hero on an art-directed backdrop (cage canvas, locker-room bench, concrete, smoke), minimal or no text.
- Copy pattern: none, or brand mark plus one line; persuasion sits in the caption (as with Summersalt / Andie no-overlay images) — [Top Growth Marketing](https://topgrowthmarketing.com/paid-ads-library/fashion-apparel-ads-that-are-actually-working-right-now/)
- Angle / funnel: aesthetic desire and brand world; cold.
- Evidence: [DATA] "Stylized product shot" is **#3 by hit rate in Fashion & Apparel** and "Product shot" #6 — [Motion CB26 formats by vertical](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/visual-formats-by-vertical). [DATA, Meta-reported] campaigns using Advantage+ creative image generation averaged 11% higher CTR and 7.6% higher conversion rate; Ben & Jerry's (non-apparel) got +7% link clicks from catalog ads with generated backgrounds — [Meta, 22 Apr 2025](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/the-creative-advantage-unlocking-the-power-of-diversification-with-meta-andromeda). Cautionary apparel case: an agency case study for Cloth and Company notes AI-generated Meta creatives "often clashed with the brand's aesthetic" [SNIPPET] — [Mavlers case study](https://api.mavlers.com/case-studies/cloth-and-company/)
- Mistakes: AI "relighting" or re-rendering the garment itself; lighting/shadow mismatch that makes the composite look pasted.
- Real stills + code only: **Yes, with care** — generate the background alone, composite the untouched cutout on top in code, add a code-drawn contact shadow. Never let the model output the garment layer.

**S15. "X reasons why" listicle (and four-panel "checkerboard")**
- Looks like: numbered list of 3–5 short reasons beside or below the product; or a 2×2 grid alternating copy and imagery (headline / product / benefit callout / usage image) — [Admetrics](https://www.admetrics.io/post/five-static-ad-formats-outperforming-video-in-2026)
- Copy pattern: "4 reasons why [product] works for [specific audience]" — [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/100m-meta-ads-5-formats-dtc); celebrity example "HERE ARE THE 4 REASONS I ONLY USE HEXCLAD IN MY KITCHENS" (non-apparel) — [Motion talk](https://runneth.com/library/talk/we-analyzed-500k-in-meta-ads-to-find-hooks-formats-that-always-win/)
- Angle / funnel: skimmable value stack; cold for credibility, warm for consideration — [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/100m-meta-ads-5-formats-dtc)
- Evidence: [PRACT] one of Howland's five core formats — [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/100m-meta-ads-5-formats-dtc); as a hook tactic "Reasons Why" sits at the bottom (25th) of Motion's top-25 spend-use list — [Motion CB26 hook tactics](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-hook-tactics). Admetrics names apparel among best-fit categories for the checkerboard but cites no data — [Admetrics](https://www.admetrics.io/post/five-static-ad-formats-outperforming-video-in-2026)
- Mistakes: more than 5 reasons; reasons that are features, not outcomes.
- Real stills + code only: **Yes.**

**S16. Before/after and "what I ordered vs what I got"**
- Looks like: vertical or horizontal split; for apparel the honest versions are "after 30 washes" (old hoodie vs this one), "expectation vs reality", or site image vs customer photo.
- Copy pattern: "What I Ordered / What I Got" split with the same tracksuit on model and customer — [Savannah Sanchez, Motion summit, Jul 2026](https://runneth.com/library/talk/15-proven-ugc-ad-hooks-that-are-working-right-now-savannah-sanchez/)
- Angle / funnel: proof against the category's main fear (item will not match the photo); cold and consideration.
- Evidence: [PRACT] Cooper groups before-vs-after with comparison ads — [Alex Cooper](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/p/how-to-make-better-static-ads-in-2026). In Motion's data "Transformation" leads in Health & Wellness, not Fashion — [Motion CB26 formats by vertical](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/visual-formats-by-vertical)
- Mistakes: simulating wear, shrinkage or fading with AI or editing (this alters the product and is deceptive).
- Real stills + code only: **Partly** — only if a real customer photo or real washed-garment photo exists.

**S17. Founder note / letter**
- Looks like: a typed or handwritten letter on paper texture, 60–120 words, signed; optionally a small product photo. Motion: "Letter — personal brand message" — [Motion format library](https://runneth.com/library/formats/)
- Copy pattern: why the product exists, one concrete decision ("we went to 350gsm because…"), sign-off.
- Angle / funnel: authenticity and small-brand underdog trust; cold and warm.
- Evidence: [DATA] Letter has the second-highest spend use ratio of all formats (1.87); Founder ads exceed 8% hit rate — [Motion talk](https://runneth.com/library/talk/we-analyzed-500k-in-meta-ads-to-find-hooks-formats-that-always-win/). Letter and Founder are top-10 in Health & Wellness; neither is in Fashion & Apparel's top 10 — [Motion CB26 formats by vertical](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/visual-formats-by-vertical)
- Mistakes: fabricated founder stories (explicitly called out as non-compliant — [adlibrary.com guide](https://adlibrary.com/guides/native-image-ads-guide)); corporate tone.
- Real stills + code only: **Yes** (text is the creative).

**S18. "Sold out / restocked" scarcity and drop announcement**
- Looks like: product hero with a stamp or banner ("BACK IN STOCK", "RESTOCKED", "DROP 02 — LIVE FRIDAY 6PM"), short outcome sub-line.
- Copy pattern: "Back in Stock! Grab Yours While Supplies Last!" plus an outcome sub-head (Arrae, non-apparel) — [Brick by Brick](https://brick-by-brick.beehiiv.com/p/these-6-statics-are-printing-money-right-now-steal-them); "…THAT KEEPS SELLING OUT" (UNTUCKit), "sold out 5X, back in a limited print" (Knix caption), restock language + "From $24" (Gymshark Women) — [Top Growth Marketing](https://topgrowthmarketing.com/paid-ads-library/fashion-apparel-ads-that-are-actually-working-right-now/)
- Angle / funnel: scarcity doubles as social proof; warm, retargeting, launches.
- Evidence: [DATA] the highest-hit-rate hook tactics are Newness 11.37%, Sale announcement 11.35%, Price anchor 10.89%, Urgency 9.73%, Announcement 9.59%, FOMO 9.20% — [Motion talk](https://runneth.com/library/talk/we-analyzed-500k-in-meta-ads-to-find-hooks-formats-that-always-win/). [PRACT] Brick by Brick advises keeping back-in-stock ads in evergreen rotation — [Brick by Brick](https://brick-by-brick.beehiiv.com/p/these-6-statics-are-printing-money-right-now-steal-them)
- Mistakes: false scarcity; restock claims on items that never sold out.
- Real stills + code only: **Yes.**

**S19. Price-anchor / cost-per-wear**
- Looks like: struck-through reference price or "what you pay elsewhere" versus brand price; or a simple equation ("$110 ÷ 200 wears = 55c").
- Copy pattern: "Rent any 6 styles for $98/month…" where "the offer is the ad" (nuuly); price-floor headline "From $24" (Gymshark) — [Top Growth Marketing](https://topgrowthmarketing.com/paid-ads-library/fashion-apparel-ads-that-are-actually-working-right-now/)
- Angle / funnel: reframes a premium price; consideration and retargeting.
- Evidence: [DATA] "Price anchor" is the #3 hook by hit rate (10.89%) and #2 by spend use (2.00) across verticals — [Motion talk](https://runneth.com/library/talk/we-analyzed-500k-in-meta-ads-to-find-hooks-formats-that-always-win/). I found no data on cost-per-wear framing specifically.
- Mistakes: invented reference prices; maths the viewer has to work out.
- Real stills + code only: **Yes.**

**S20. Size-guide / fit reassurance**
- Looks like: on-model photo with height/weight/size worn labels, or a simple size table beside the flat-lay; "true to size" badge.
- Copy pattern: checkable range replaces adjective ("Every style in sizes 00-40"); cropping the face so "any body can picture the fit" (Gymshark) — [Top Growth Marketing](https://topgrowthmarketing.com/paid-ads-library/fashion-apparel-ads-that-are-actually-working-right-now/)
- Angle / funnel: removes the biggest apparel objection; retargeting / cart abandoners.
- Evidence: examples only. Motion catalogues "Try-On" as a format and it is 12% of Gym Glamour's library [SNIPPET] — [Motion Gym Glamour](https://motionapp.com/library/gym-glamour). No performance data found for size-guide statics.
- Mistakes: AI try-on that redraws the garment on a synthetic body (fidelity violation); dense tables unreadable on mobile.
- Real stills + code only: **Partly** — needs real on-model photos with real model measurements.

**S21. Meme / unconventional text placement / billboard and sign mockups**
- Looks like: (a) meme template with product as punchline; (b) headline placed somewhere unexpected — on a label, a receipt, a fight poster, a whiteboard; (c) the ad shown as a billboard or hand-held sign. Motion definitions: "Meme — meme-style parody", "Unconventional Text Placement — text placed in an unexpected spot", "Billboard — outdoor billboard-style ad", "Sign — handheld or posted sign" — [Motion format library](https://runneth.com/library/formats/)
- Copy pattern: in-group humour ("cultural fluency"); short.
- Angle / funnel: entertainment and identity signalling; cold.
- Evidence: [DATA] in Fashion & Apparel, Meme is #4 by hit rate; Unconventional text placement is #10 by hit rate and #2 by spend use; Billboard #3 and Sign #5 by spend use — [Motion CB26 formats by vertical](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/visual-formats-by-vertical); Unconventional text placement spend use 1.44 across verticals — [Motion talk](https://runneth.com/library/talk/we-analyzed-500k-in-meta-ads-to-find-hooks-formats-that-always-win/). [PRACT] Admetrics' "anti-ad" and "clever one" describe the same family, uncited — [Admetrics](https://www.admetrics.io/post/five-static-ad-formats-outperforming-video-in-2026)
- Mistakes: stale memes; third-party IP (fighter likenesses, promotion logos, copyrighted meme images).
- Real stills + code only: **Yes** for text-placement and typographic memes; **Partly** for billboard/sign mockups (the scene would be AI-generated, with the real ad composited flat onto it).

**S22. Catalog / DPA with overlays ("enriched" catalog)**
- Looks like: the catalogue product image inside a branded frame — price, badge ("Restocked", "350gsm"), star rating, colour-way count — generated per SKU.
- Angle / funnel: automated mid/lower funnel and retargeting.
- Evidence: [DATA, agency-compiled] CTC (6 May 2026): top performers generate 60% of Meta revenue from Catalog Ads; Catalog Ads show 23% higher ROAS and 37% better CPA; accounts allocating 60–100% of budget to them see 44% higher ROAS and 68% lower CPA — [Common Thread Collective](https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/meta-andromeda-roas-creative-strategy-2026). [Vendor, observed ranges, Mar 2026] fashion "Enriched DPA" 1.0–1.6% CTR and 4–8.1x ROAS versus "Static catalog" 0.5–0.9% CTR and 1–2.5x; enrichment lifts ROAS 73–115% over generic catalogs — [QuickAds](https://www.quickads.ai/d2c-fashion-creative-intelligence). [DATA, TikTok case study] New Look: Carousel + Video catalog ads gave 61% higher ROAS than video-only catalog ads — [TikTok for Business](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/inspiration/new-look-tiktok-success-story)
- Mistakes: overlays hiding the product; letting platform generative features rewrite the product image.
- Real stills + code only: **Yes** (template in code; disable generative image enhancements).

**S23. Text-only**
- Looks like: solid colour or paper background, one statement in large type, no product (or a thumbnail).
- Angle / funnel: maximum pattern interrupt, cheapest test of an angle; cold.
- Evidence: [DATA] "Text only" ranks **#1 of 15 asset types on both hit rate and spend use** across verticals — [Motion CB26 top asset types](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-asset-types). Not broken out for apparel.
- Mistakes: for apparel, never showing the product anywhere in the ad unit.
- Real stills + code only: **Yes.**

**S24. Quiz / "which one are you?"**
- Looks like: a question headline with 2–4 labelled options, each a product or colourway ("Which walkout are you? A / B / C").
- Angle / funnel: self-identification and comment-bait; cold.
- Evidence: [DATA] **#2 by hit rate in Fashion & Apparel**; Motion defines it as "interactive question-and-answer format" — [Motion CB26 formats by vertical](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/visual-formats-by-vertical), [Motion format library](https://runneth.com/library/formats/). No example ads or values published.
- Mistakes: options that are not visually distinct; no path from answer to product.
- Real stills + code only: **Yes.**

### Inferences

- For apparel, the formats most often recommended by generalist DTC sources (us-vs-them, testimonial stack, before/after, reasons-why) come mainly from supplement / skincare / gadget accounts. The only vertical-specific dataset points apparel toward **native-feeling, low-effort, aesthetic and humour formats** (post-it, quiz, meme, social comment, stylized product shot). Both groups are worth testing, but the first group should not be assumed to transfer.
- Nearly every top apparel static is "real product photo + typography + a simple overlay", which means the fidelity constraint (never regenerate the garment) costs this brand almost nothing on the static side. The only static formats that genuinely need assets the brand may not have are S4 (press), S16 (real after-wash or customer photo) and S20 (on-model with measurements).
- Because Motion's "winner" definition is spend-based, a format's hit rate measures how often Meta's delivery system chooses to scale it within an account, not profitability. Offer-led formats in particular will look better in that data than they are for margin.
- Native UI mimics (S6–S9) are a family of interchangeable skins on the same idea; under Andromeda-style similarity grouping (see Key Question 3) five UI skins carrying the same message are likely to behave as one concept.

### Gaps

- No source gives numeric hit rates for Fashion & Apparel formats; Motion publishes rank order only on the web pages (values are said to be in the PDF, pages 15–20, which I could not access).
- No performance data found for Apple Notes, Google-search-bar, ChatGPT-style, calendar/reminder, Reddit-thread or tweet mockups in apparel specifically.
- No data found on cost-per-wear framing, size-guide statics, or founder-letter statics in apparel.
- Whether Meta policy restricts mimicking platform or third-party UI (iMessage, ChatGPT, TikTok comment bubbles) was not established from a primary Meta policy page; the one guide consulted says to check Meta's current policies directly — [adlibrary.com guide](https://adlibrary.com/guides/native-image-ads-guide).
- Foreplay, Atria, Pilothouse, Thesis, Marpipe, Barry Hott, Dara Denney, Sarah Levinger, Mirella Crespi, Marcus Burke, Cody Plofker and Reddit threads were in the suggested source list; searches did not surface usable, dated, apparel-specific material from them within the time available (Foreplay's format directory lists names only — [Foreplay formats](https://www.foreplay.co/adlibrary/formats)).

## Key Question 2 — Which short-video formats work for apparel, and which can be made without filming talent?

### Takeaway

The video formats with the strongest apparel-specific signal are **ASMR (#5 by hit rate), Podcast (#1 by spend use), Slideshow (#7 by spend use) and Demo (#10 by spend use)**; of these, only Slideshow is fully buildable from stills. Motion-graphics-heavy video is a weak bet on the evidence available — "Animation" ranks second-last of 15 asset types — so no-talent video should look like native slideshows, shuffles and screen recordings rather than polished explainers, and a short phone-shot fabric clip would unlock the highest-value format the brand cannot fake.

### Cited Findings

**A. Cross-format evidence**

- [DATA] Fashion & Apparel video-capable formats in Motion's top 10s: ASMR (#5 hit rate), Podcast (#8 hit rate, #1 spend use), Slideshow (#7 spend use), Celebrity (#6 spend use), Demo (#10 spend use) — [Motion CB26 formats by vertical](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/visual-formats-by-vertical)
- [DATA] Across verticals: Unboxing has the highest hit rate (9.83%), then Offer-first banner 8.68%, Demo 8.11%; Behind-the-scenes and Founder both exceed 8%; POV has a high hit rate but the lowest spend use ratio. Motion labels offer-first banners and demos "scale formats", unboxing and POV "volatile formats", celebrity "big swings" — [Motion talk, Apr 2026](https://runneth.com/library/talk/we-analyzed-500k-in-meta-ads-to-find-hooks-formats-that-always-win/)
- [DATA] Asset types: UGC ranks 4th on hit rate and spend use; High production 5th / 10th; GIF 6th / 11th; UGC mashup 8th / 7th; Hybrid 12th / 8th; **Animation 14th / 14th; Carousel 15th / 15th** — [Motion CB26 top asset types](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-asset-types)
- [Vendor, observed ranges, Mar 2026, 400+ fashion ads] Raw UGC/Reels CTR 1.8–3.0% (top-of-funnel only); Education video CTR 1.5–2.2%, ROAS 3–6x, low saturation; fabric/tech education (Vuori, Allbirds) 3–5x cold ROAS; US top-performing fashion ads are 70–80% UGC with polished studio under 20% — [QuickAds](https://www.quickads.ai/d2c-fashion-creative-intelligence)
- [PRACT, vendor] Faceless ads make the voice the presenter, so a flat robotic read hurts credibility — [NovoAds](https://novoads.ai/en/blog/faceless-video-ads); stop-motion and flat-lay product shots are a standard faceless format, and voiceover should be tested against text-only — [Faysell](https://faysell.vercel.app/blogs/faceless-video-ads-guide)
- [PRACT, single anecdote] one Ads Manager comparison showed a person-led UGC ad at 6.2x ROAS versus 0.8x for a product-only ad — [InVideo](https://invideo.io/faq/faceless-ugc-ads-vs-full-character-ugc-ads-which/)
- [PRACT] Of 15 "working right now" hooks shown by Savannah Sanchez (examples pulled late June 2026), 6 need no face on camera (hand/product/text only) — [Savannah Sanchez, Motion summit](https://runneth.com/library/talk/15-proven-ugc-ad-hooks-that-are-working-right-now-savannah-sanchez/)
- [PRACT] Segwise/Howland: demo videos 20–40 s; problem-solution best at 30–45 s; hook rate (3-second views ÷ impressions) below 25% = kill, 30–40% = scale — [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/100m-meta-ads-5-formats-dtc)

**B. Video format cards (V1–V17)**

Pacing notes assume 9:16, 1080×1920, sound-on with burned-in captions.

**V1. Kinetic-typography product explainer with voiceover**

- Angle / funnel: clarity of one argument; cold and consideration.
- Evidence: no direct performance data found. Positive adjacent signal: text-led assets top the table — same source.

**V2. Stop-motion / flat-lay shuffle**

Motion catalogues "Stop Motion — frame-by-frame animation" and "Flatlay" — [Motion format library](https://runneth.com/library/formats/)

### Inferences

- **Can a 3D spin be faked safely from stills?** No source addresses this; my assessment is no. A flat-lay or ghost shot contains one or two viewpoints; any tool that produces a rotating garment must invent the side and three-quarter views, including how the print wraps and how seams and fabric fall — precisely the regeneration the brief forbids. Safe substitutes: (1) a card-flip between the real front and real back photo; (2) 2D rotation/scale of the cutout in-plane; (3) layer parallax between the untouched cutout and a separate background; (4) a real turntable sequence shot on a phone (12–24 photos) played as stop-motion.
- Ken Burns is safe only as an affine transform (pan/zoom/rotate) of the original pixels. "AI depth parallax" or image-to-video tools re-synthesise pixels inside the garment and should be treated as altering the product.
- Ranking no-talent video by evidence: V10 Slideshow (platform data + apparel spend-use ranking) > V16 animated static and V5 text-message (inherit apparel static evidence) > V14 drop teaser (hook-tactic evidence) > V2/V3/V4 (plausible, lightly evidenced) > V1 kinetic explainer and V13 spin (weakest evidence; Animation under-performs).
- The two highest-value formats that cannot be built from stills (V8 ASMR/fabric macro and V11 unboxing) need only a phone, hands and ten minutes. That is a much smaller ask than "filming talent" and would cover apparel's #5 format and the overall #1 hit-rate format.

### Gaps

- No dataset isolates no-talent / faceless video performance for apparel; the evidence is vendor commentary plus one contrary anecdote.
- No sourced optimal duration per video format for apparel in 2026; duration guidance found is generic (see Key Question 4).
- No evidence found, positive or negative, on AI-generated b-roll hooks or on faux-3D product spins for apparel ads.
- No TikTok Creative Center format-level performance data for apparel was retrieved.

## Key Question 3 — What does current data say about static vs video share and performance for apparel on Meta, and about creative volume and concept diversity vs iterations (Andromeda / GEM / Advantage+)?

### Takeaway

No authoritative 2026 source publishes a clean apparel static-vs-video spend split; the fragments available suggest apparel leans more static than other DTC categories (fashion "static 60%" among surviving winners; Gymshark's library 78% image), while UGC video dominates top-of-funnel. On volume, the consistent message from Meta and practitioners is that **distinct concepts matter and near-duplicates are grouped together**; at under US$10K a month the benchmark is roughly 3 new creatives a week, a 4% hit rate, and 8–12 genuinely different concepts a month.

### Cited Findings

**Static vs video**

- [DATA, relayed] A Brandsearch analysis of 523 "winning/surviving" Meta creatives found video dominated survivors in fitness (78%), home goods (68%) and supplements (62%), while **static won in fashion (60%)** — reported by [Segwise, Jun 2026, updated Sep 2026](https://segwise.ai/blog/why-boring-ads-beat-polished-ads). I could not locate the primary Brandsearch study; the Brandsearch article I opened (15 Nov 2026) does not contain it — [Brandsearch](https://brandsearch.co/blog/winning-meta-ad-patterns-2026).
- [SNIPPET] Gymshark's Meta library (250 ads, 29 Jan–28 Feb 2026): 78% image, 22% video — [GoMarble](https://www.gomarble.ai/library/brands/gymshark/)
- [Vendor, Mar 2026] Observed spend mixes: Gymshark UGC 50% / lifestyle-carousel 25% / DPA-catalog 20%; Skims UGC 45% / carousel-lifestyle 30% / DPA 20%; Gymshark runs 100–300+ ads at once — [QuickAds](https://www.quickads.ai/d2c-fashion-creative-intelligence). This conflicts in emphasis with the GoMarble count above (ad count vs estimated spend share are different measures).
- [DATA] Motion CB26 does not publish a static-vs-video split; its asset-type ranking puts Text only and Product image with text above UGC and High production on hit rate — [Motion CB26 top asset types](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-asset-types)
- [PRACT] CTC does not rank static against video; it recommends covering video (UGC, polished brand, talking head), static (lifestyle, product-forward, infographic) and catalog together — [Common Thread Collective, 6 May 2026](https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/meta-andromeda-roas-creative-strategy-2026)
- [PRACT, uncited claim] Admetrics asserts static outperforms video in a "significant share" of campaigns and that a static angle gives directional signal in ~72 hours, suggesting $1,000–$2,000 spend before promoting a static concept to video; no data or methodology is given — [Admetrics, 16 Apr 2026](https://www.admetrics.io/post/five-static-ad-formats-outperforming-video-in-2026)
- [SNIPPET, undated] A Varos fashion analysis says high performers run Video 32% and Image 34% and are "more diversified in their creative mix"; the page returned 404 when opened — [Varos](https://webflow.varos.com/blog/fashion-companies-meta-ads-facebook-instagram-analysis-with-varos)

**Creative volume, winners and spend tier**

- [DATA] About 5% of creatives are winners; winners capture 55% of total spend (23% at Micro tier rising to 64% at Enterprise) — [Motion CB26 overview](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/)
- [DATA] By monthly Meta spend: Micro (<$10K) 2.8 creatives tested per week, 4.0% hit rate; Small ($10–50K) 4.1 / 6.4%; Medium ($50–200K) 6.6 / 8.1%; Large ($200K–1M) 11.2 / 8.6%; Enterprise ($1M+) 18.8 / 8.8%. Top-quartile accounts test roughly 1.5–3x their tier average. "Volume is the dominant lever behind winner production at scale" — [Motion CB26 testing volume by tier](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/testing-volume-by-tier)
- [DATA, agency-compiled] Brands testing 20+ new ads a month saw 65% higher ROAS than those testing fewer than 10 (CTC portfolio plus a third-party study of 1M+ creatives; correlational) — [Common Thread Collective](https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/meta-andromeda-roas-creative-strategy-2026)
- [PRACT] CTC volume guidance: under $10K/month 8–12 new, conceptually distinct creatives a month; $10–50K 15–25; $50K+ 25–40+; fatigue window now 2–4 weeks — [Common Thread Collective](https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/meta-andromeda-roas-creative-strategy-2026)
- [PRACT] Howland: one new concept per $3,000 monthly spend; run 8–15 conceptually different ads; wait 5–7 days before judging — [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/100m-meta-ads-5-formats-dtc)
- [PRACT, SNIPPET] An Australian agency suggests 3–5 genuinely different creatives for most small businesses — [Excite Media](https://www.excitemedia.com.au/meta-creative-diversification/); another vendor warns 20 ads on a small budget can fragment delivery — [Hunch](https://www.hunchads.com/blog/meta-andromeda-explained)
- [DATA, TikTok, likely 2024] Over 60% of TikTok advertisers see a positive correlation between number of creatives and campaign effectiveness — [TikTok Image Ads Playbook](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/Image_Ads_Carousel_Ads_Playbook.pdf)

**Meta's own guidance; concepts vs iterations**

- [Meta, 22 Apr 2025] Creative diversification is "creating a wide range of ad creatives with different themes, messages, and visuals"; "the focus has shifted from niche targeting to creative diversification as the best lever"; "the biggest constraint you can put on your Meta strategy is the constraints you place on your creative process" — [Meta for Business](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/the-creative-advantage-unlocking-the-power-of-diversification-with-meta-andromeda), quote also reproduced by [Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/meta-andromeda/)
- [Meta-reported, Apr 2025] Advantage+ sales campaigns with streamlined setup: 9% lower cost per action on average; Andromeda: 8% improvement in ads quality; Dribbleup went from 3–4 to almost 50 new creatives a week while holding performance — [Meta for Business](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/the-creative-advantage-unlocking-the-power-of-diversification-with-meta-andromeda)
- [Meta, Mar 2025, via Loomer] Andromeda is a retrieval system introduced in 2024 with "a 10,000x increase in the complexity of models used for ads retrieval"; it narrows tens of millions of ads to a few thousand candidates per person — [Jon Loomer, 19 Aug 2025](https://www.jonloomer.com/meta-andromeda/)
- [Secondary report of a Meta post dated Dec 2025; primary not opened] Meta states "creative diversification and creative iteration are two separate concepts"; iteration can inform new concepts but should not replace diversification; Meta publishes no fixed weekly number of ads — [New Engen](https://newengen.com/insights/meta-creative-diversification/), [Knowlify](https://knowlify.com/articles/meta-andromeda-creative)
- [PRACT] Loomer: Meta's old "no more than six ads per ad set" recommendation disappeared in February 2025; he recommends diversifying concepts, angles, formats and personas rather than minor tweaks, and warns 50 ads guarantee nothing — [Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/meta-andromeda/)
- [PRACT] CTC: Andromeda groups visually similar ads into one "entity", so "run 10 variations of the same static product shot and they don't get 10 chances at distribution"; top accounts keep creative similarity below 40%; bottom performers run 3–5 variations of the same ad — [Common Thread Collective](https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/meta-andromeda-roas-creative-strategy-2026)
- [DATA, third-party via CTC] In a study of 3,014 advertisers, ROAS fell 7% on average during the Andromeda rollout; a single ad set with 25 diverse creatives delivered 17% more conversions at 16% lower cost than five narrow ad sets — [Common Thread Collective](https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/meta-andromeda-roas-creative-strategy-2026)

### Inferences

- For a brand almost certainly in Motion's Micro tier, the realistic plan is ~3 new creatives a week and an expectation that roughly 1 in 25 becomes a scaled winner. The catalogue above should therefore be used to make each of those few slots a **different concept** (different format family × different angle), not five colour variants of one layout.
- A small catalogue of flat-lay shots is a similarity risk: ten ads built on the same hoodie photo with different headlines may be clustered as one entity. Varying the visual structure (post-it vs comment bubble vs AI-scene vs text-only vs slideshow) is the available lever for diversity when the product photography is fixed.
- The apparel static lean plus the strength of catalog ads suggests a workable low-budget structure: a handful of distinct statics for prospecting, an enriched catalog for retargeting, and one or two still-based videos to occupy Reels/TikTok placements.

### Gaps

- No Triple Whale, Varos, Pilothouse or Foreplay 2026 figure for apparel static-vs-video share of spend was found; Triple Whale's 2025 Apparel & Accessories benchmark report exists but was not opened — [Triple Whale](https://www.triplewhale.com/reports-guides/fashion-industry-benchmarks-2025).
- The Brandsearch 523-ad study (fashion static 60%) is known only second-hand; sample size per niche and the "winning" definition are unverified.
- Meta's December 2025 diversification-vs-iteration post was not read at source. No Meta document specifically about GEM-era creative guidance was retrieved. I found no official Meta definition of an "Entity ID" or a published similarity threshold; the 40% figure is CTC's.
- No Australia-specific benchmarks were found.

## Key Question 4 — What aspect ratios, safe zones, text lengths and durations are recommended per placement (Meta Feed, Reels/Stories, TikTok)?

### Takeaway

Build two masters per concept: **4:5 (1080×1350, or 1440×1800) for Meta feed** and **9:16 (1080×1920) for Reels, Stories and TikTok**, keeping all product and text inside the central band — roughly the area below the top 14% and above the bottom 35% on Meta, and inside TikTok's much tighter template (about 240 px top, 660 px bottom, 120 px sides, plus a right-hand button column). Safe-zone figures come from third-party measurement, not platform-published numbers, so previews must be checked.

### Cited Findings

**Meta**

- "Vertical 4:5 is recommended for single-image ads to be delivered to the ad placement Facebook Feed" (Meta best-practices documentation as quoted); sizes 1080×1350 minimum, 1440×1800 high-res; Instagram Feed 4:5 for image and 4:5 or 9:16 for video — [Ads Uploader, updated 31 Jul 2026](https://adsuploader.com/blog/meta-ads-aspect-ratios)
- Stories, Reels (Facebook and Instagram) and WhatsApp Status: 9:16, 1080×1920 minimum, 1440×2560 high-res (attributed to Meta help centre) — [Ads Uploader](https://adsuploader.com/blog/meta-ads-aspect-ratios)
- Marketplace 1:1; right column 1.91:1 (1200×628); Threads feed 4:5 image / 9:16 video; 4:5 now supported for carousel cards in Feed; Instagram profile grid moved to 3:4 previews in 2025 — [Ads Uploader](https://adsuploader.com/blog/meta-ads-aspect-ratios)
- Unified 9:16 safe zone for Stories and Reels (2026): keep clear the **top 14%, bottom 35%, and 6% each side**; on tall 20:9 phones keep critical content in the centre ~80% horizontally. These percentages are the article's, not cited to a Meta document, and the article is internally inconsistent on the bottom figure (896 px vs 672 px on a 1440×2560 canvas) — [Ads Uploader](https://adsuploader.com/blog/meta-ads-aspect-ratios)
- [SNIPPET] Primary text truncates at about 125 characters in feed; other blogs give 50–150 for Facebook feed and an uncorroborated 72 characters for Reels — [Hawky](https://hawky.ai/blog/facebook-ad-sizes), [First Pier](https://www.firstpier.com/resources/facebook-ad-sizes)
- [PRACT] Start from 9:16 vertical to cover Reels, Stories and Feed — [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/100m-meta-ads-5-formats-dtc); on-image message of ten words or fewer — [Admetrics](https://www.admetrics.io/post/five-static-ad-formats-outperforming-video-in-2026)

**TikTok**

- Official In-Feed (non-Spark) specs: 9:16 recommended, minimum 540×960 (also 1:1 ≥640×640, 16:9 ≥960×540); ≤500 MB; bitrate ≥516 kbps; auction video up to 10 minutes; Spark Ads captions show a maximum of 4 lines; no fixed safe-zone numbers — TikTok says safe zone "depends on the aspect ratio, caption length, and any additional formats" and provides downloadable template files — [TikTok Ads Help Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/video-ads-specifications?lang=en)
- Third-party measurement of TikTok's 1080×1920 template: top 240 px, bottom 660 px, left/right 120 px, plus a lower-right button column 180 px wide extending 1,080 px up; worst case with anchor and four-line caption: bottom 1,014 px. Stay under ~100 characters of caption to avoid "See more"; TopView / Reach & Frequency 5–60 s with 9–15 s recommended — [ManyPixels, 11 Nov 2026](https://www.manypixels.co/blog/social-media-design/tiktok-ad-specs)
- TikTok carousel / image ads: 2–35 images (catalog VSA carousel 2–20); JPG/PNG; vertical 720×1280 (also 640×640, 1200×628); music required, ≥2 s; one caption, one CTA and one URL for all images; Standard Carousel does not support Spark Ads; TikTok recommends 9:16, 3 or 7–9 images, 720p+, content inside safe zone, "Smart Order" on — [TikTok Image Ads Playbook, undated, likely 2024](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/Image_Ads_Carousel_Ads_Playbook.pdf)

**Durations (practitioner)**

- Demo 20–40 s; problem-solution under 30 s to 30–45 s; fatigue typically weeks 3–4 in prospecting — [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/100m-meta-ads-5-formats-dtc)
- Fashion concepts observed at 15–30 s (UGC) and 30 s (education reel) [vendor] — [QuickAds](https://www.quickads.ai/d2c-fashion-creative-intelligence)
- A 7-second single-product clip is cited as a top-engagement apparel ad — [Brandsearch](https://brandsearch.co/blog/winning-meta-ad-patterns-2026)

### Inferences

- A single "universal safe box" that satisfies both Meta 9:16 and TikTok is approximately x 120–900 px, y 270–1,250 px on a 1080×1920 canvas (derived by intersecting the Meta percentages with the ManyPixels TikTok measurements, and avoiding TikTok's right-hand button column for small text). Product cutout and headline should sit inside it; backgrounds can bleed.
- For still-based video, 6–15 s is the defensible range: long enough for one idea, consistent with TikTok's 9–15 s reserved-format recommendation and the 7-second apparel example, and short enough that limited photography does not repeat.
- Export statics at 1440×1800 / 1440×2560 where the source product photo has the resolution; never upscale the garment with AI to reach those sizes (upscaling re-synthesises texture).

### Gaps

- Meta's own safe-zone page and current character limits were not read at source; all Meta safe-zone numbers here are third-party and partly inconsistent.
- No platform-published recommended duration for TikTok auction in-feed ads was found; the widely repeated "21–34 seconds" figure appeared only in non-TikTok blogs and is unverified.
- "Late 2026" changes beyond those listed (e.g. any Q4 2026 spec update) could not be confirmed.

## Key Question 5 — What hook patterns (first line / first 2 seconds) work for hoodies, sports jerseys and combat-sports / gym audiences? Real example lines.

### Takeaway

The data-backed hook families are **newness/drop, price anchor, urgency/FOMO and announcement** (9–11% hit rates), with "interrupt" hooks (confession, contrarian, warning) lower but steadier outside sale periods; question hooks are reported weakest. Real apparel hook lines are overwhelmingly short, literal benefit comparisons ("softer than a blanket", "never iron again"). I found **no published hook evidence specific to MMA / fightwear**, and only thin, vendor-level material for gym apparel.

### Cited Findings

**Hook-tactic data (all verticals, Meta)**

- Hit rate: Newness 11.37%, Sale announcement 11.35%, Price anchor 10.89%, Urgency 9.73%, Announcement 9.59%, Offer only 9.29%, FOMO 9.20%; Bold claim 7.19% — [Motion talk, Apr 2026](https://runneth.com/library/talk/we-analyzed-500k-in-meta-ads-to-find-hooks-formats-that-always-win/)
- Spend use ratio: Giveaway 2.29, Price anchor 2.00, Announcement 1.57, Event announcement 1.57, Offer only 1.42, Urgency 1.31, FOMO 1.22 — [Motion talk](https://runneth.com/library/talk/we-analyzed-500k-in-meta-ads-to-find-hooks-formats-that-always-win/)
- Full top-25 by hit rate continues: New product announcement, Confession, Exclusivity, Curiosity, Giveaway, Event announcement, Bold claim, Reverse psychology, Shocking statement, If/Then, Warning, Wordplay, Contrarian, Relatability, Contrast, Direct address, Product announcement, Authority. Motion separates a "promotional cluster" (why act now) from an "interrupt cluster" (Confession, Contrarian, Shocking statement, Warning) and says promotional hooks are probably over-represented because of BFCM — [Motion CB26 hook tactics](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-hook-tactics)
- [Relayed, primary not found] In 523 surviving Meta ads, pain-point opener, authority claim and visual pattern interrupt made up 71% of 30+ day survivors; question hooks opened only 8% — [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/why-boring-ads-beat-polished-ads)
- [PRACT] Hook types for the first 3 seconds: problem-first; qualifying ("If you're a [specific person], keep watching"); results-first; pattern interrupt; "Stop doing X". The same creative with a different hook can perform 2–3x differently — [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/100m-meta-ads-5-formats-dtc)
- [PRACT] Static ads "have hooks too": decide what is seen first; emotive, audience-qualifying headlines taken from reviews have spent six figures (non-apparel examples) — [Alex Cooper](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/p/how-to-make-better-static-ads-in-2026)
- [SNIPPET, vendor] Hook-rate norms quoted at ~28% on Meta and ~33% on TikTok; TikTok hooks should sound spoken rather than written — [Segwise hooks post](https://segwise.ai/blog/dtc-ad-creative-hooks.md)

**Real hook lines from live apparel ads (published Aug–Nov 2026; no performance figures)** — all from [Top Growth Marketing swipe file](https://topgrowthmarketing.com/paid-ads-library/fashion-apparel-ads-that-are-actually-working-right-now/)

- Hoodie: "THIS HOODIE IS SOFTER THAN A BLANKET" + headline "Introducing Quilted Rib" (Mack Weldon) — comparison to a familiar reference plus a named proprietary fabric.
- Sports / licensed: "OFFICIALLY LICENSED PRODUCT" / "THE ULTIMATE FAN UNIFORM" (Chubbies) — reframes shorts as game-day kit, with a fabric close-up.

### Inferences

Candidate hook lines for a fightwear brand, mapped to the evidenced patterns (these are my adaptations, untested):

- Comparison-to-familiar (Mack Weldon pattern): "This hoodie weighs more than your gloves."
- Identity contrast ("X are for posers" pattern): "Gym hoodies are for the gym. This one's for the walkout."
- Reframe as kit (Chubbies pattern): "Your fight-night uniform."
- Spec as number (S11): "350gsm. Feel the difference."
- Newness / drop (top hook tactic): "Drop 02 is live." / "Restocked — last run sold out in X days" (only if true).
- "Actually" concession (Vessi pattern): "Actually heavyweight."
- Qualifier: "If you train, you already know what a bad hoodie feels like."
- Fandom/drop timing: tie releases to major fight weekends, since fandom drops are reported strong at launch but short-lived.

The fandom pattern carries IP risk: fighter names, likenesses and promotion marks should not appear without licence (the Chubbies example works because it is officially licensed).

- No hook-rate or CTR data tied to specific hoodie or jersey hook lines; all apparel lines above are observed live ads without results.

## Key Question 6 — What evidence exists on "lo-fi / ugly" ads vs polished brand ads for apparel, and on AI-generated statics vs designer-made ones?

### Takeaway

Evidence favours **low-polish, high-clarity** creative in aggregate (text-only and product-with-text beat high production on hit rate; UGC is the bulk of top fashion ads), but there is no controlled apparel study, and several sources warn that lo-fi can undercut premium positioning. On AI vs human, the only platform-published numbers are Meta's own tool lifts (+11% CTR, +7.6% conversion rate); third-party comparisons are vendor-sourced and suggest AI creative holds up below about US$100 AOV and trails human work at higher prices.

### Cited Findings

**Lo-fi vs polished**

- [DATA] Text only and Product image with text outrank High production on hit rate and spend use; high production ranks 5th and 10th respectively — [Motion CB26 top asset types](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-asset-types). Motion notes "High production" covers a wide range of quality and cost — same source.
- [DATA] Celebrity/high-production "big swings" have the highest spend use (2.17) but a 5.9% hit rate — [Motion CB26 top visual formats](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-visual-formats)
- [Vendor] In US fashion, 70–80% of top-performing ads are UGC and polished studio is under 20% — [QuickAds, Mar 2026](https://www.quickads.ai/d2c-fashion-creative-intelligence)
- [Relayed, unverified] "Smartphone-quality video beats studio production 84% of the time in Stories, per Motion's research" — [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/100m-meta-ads-5-formats-dtc); I could not find the Motion original.
- [PRACT] A qualitative table of 12 winning archetypes rates 9 as low or very-low polish and 11 as high clarity; "clarity is the constant, polish the variable" — [Segwise, Jun–Sep 2026](https://segwise.ai/blog/why-boring-ads-beat-polished-ads)
- [PRACT, **Jan 2024 — pre-2025, possibly outdated**] A Pattern Brands executive estimated that in three of four cases the uglier ad wins and scales; other marketers in the same piece disagree that ugly always wins — [Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/marketing/dtc-briefing-why-ugly-ads-are-taking-off/)
- [PRACT, counter-view] Lo-fi can undermine the aspirational positioning that justifies premium pricing in fashion — [Breef, Jun 2026](https://www.breef.com/breefingroom/articles/content-that-looks-like-an-ad-gets-treated-like-an-ad); higher production may be the next "arbitrage" as feeds fill with lo-fi — [Repeat newsletter](https://repeat.substack.com/p/produced-revisited)
- [PRACT] Apparel at the top end still runs brand-only polished creative as a control (lululemon: studio look, no text, no offer) — [Top Growth Marketing](https://topgrowthmarketing.com/paid-ads-library/fashion-apparel-ads-that-are-actually-working-right-now/)
- [PRACT] "Native" must not mean fake: no fabricated testimonials, stolen photos, invented founder stories or unedited AI faces — [adlibrary.com guide, 9 Aug 2026](https://adlibrary.com/guides/native-image-ads-guide)

**AI-generated vs human-made**

- [Meta-reported, Apr 2025] Campaigns using Advantage+ creative image generation: 11% higher CTR and 7.6% higher conversion rate on average — [Meta for Business](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/the-creative-advantage-unlocking-the-power-of-diversification-with-meta-andromeda)
- [Vendor, SNIPPET] A benchmark of 50,000+ ad variations reports ~12% CTR lift for AI creative on Meta, with AI matching or beating human creative on ROAS below US$100 AOV and human creative ahead above it — [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/ai-ad-creative-benchmark-2026-ctr-roas-data); another gives under-$25 AOV AI 4.8x vs human 4.5x, $25–100 a dead heat, and above $500 AI 2.3x vs human 3.1x — [Coinis](https://coinis.com/blog/ai-agents-took-the-wheel-meta-tiktok-google-ads). Methodologies unpublished.
- [PRACT, SNIPPET, skincare — other category] In a US$15K test, human UGC won on ROAS and AI UGC won on variants per dollar — [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/ai-ugc-vs-human-ugc-meta-ads-roas-2026)
- [PRACT] Use AI for volume, but it "should supplement your creative strategy, not replace it" — [Alex Cooper](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/p/how-to-make-better-static-ads-in-2026)
- [SNIPPET] An apparel agency case study lists "AI-generated Meta creatives often clashed with the brand's aesthetic" as a problem encountered — [Mavlers / Cloth and Company](https://api.mavlers.com/case-studies/cloth-and-company/)
- [SNIPPET, unverified] Meta "Made with AI" disclosure for photorealistic imagery since March 2026 — [Coinis](https://coinis.com/blog/ai-agents-took-the-wheel-meta-tiktok-google-ads); New York disclosure requirement for AI-generated people in ads effective 9 June (2026) — [Alex Cooper](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/p/how-to-make-better-static-ads-in-2026)

### Inferences

- At roughly AUD 80–110 (about US$50–75), the brand sits at or just under the price band where vendor data says AI-assisted creative is at parity with human creative. That is weak evidence, but it does not argue against an AI-assisted workflow.
- The fidelity-safe workflow proposed (real product pixels + code layout + AI used only for backgrounds, copy drafts and voice) is closer to "designer-made with templating" than to "AI-generated creative" as measured in those studies; the studies therefore say little about it either way. The Meta tool lift for background generation is the closest analogue.
- "Lo-fi" for a premium heavyweight product should mean low-effort *format* (post-it, comment, text-only) around a **well-shot product**, not degraded product imagery; the product photo is the quality signal.

### Gaps

- No controlled test of lo-fi vs polished for apparel was found.
- No independent, methodologically transparent 2026 study of AI-generated statics vs designer-made statics; the widely quoted figures are vendor claims. A reported Meta "AdLlama" result (+6.7% CTR across 640,000 ads) surfaced in a search summary but I could not attribute it to a source, so it is excluded.
- The AI-disclosure points are snippet-level and belong to the AI-tooling researcher to verify.

## Key Question 8 — Ranked shortlist: formats best suited to a small fightwear brand with flat-lay product photos and a no-AI-on-garment rule

### Takeaway

Lead with product-image-plus-text statics in several structurally different skins (bold claim / feature callout, post-it, social comment, drop/restock, offer bundle, AI-scene hero), back them with an enriched catalog, and use still-based slideshows and animated statics for vertical video placements. Add one short phone-shot fabric clip as soon as possible, because the best apparel video signal (ASMR) cannot be reproduced from stills.

### Cited Findings

Evidence underpinning the ranking (all detailed above):

- Apparel-specific hit-rate leaders: Post-it #1, Quiz #2, Stylized product shot #3, Meme #4, ASMR #5, Product shot #6, Social comment #7 — [Motion CB26 formats by vertical](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/visual-formats-by-vertical)
- Apparel-specific spend-use leaders include Unconventional text placement #2, Text message #4, Slideshow #7, Post-it #8, Offer-first banner #9 — [Motion CB26 formats by vertical](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/visual-formats-by-vertical)
- Text only and Product image with text are the top two asset types; Animation and Carousel the bottom two — [Motion CB26 top asset types](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-asset-types)
- Newness, price anchor, urgency and announcement are the top hook tactics — [Motion talk](https://runneth.com/library/talk/we-analyzed-500k-in-meta-ads-to-find-hooks-formats-that-always-win/)
- Micro-tier accounts average 2.8 creatives a week at a 4.0% hit rate — [Motion CB26 testing volume by tier](https://runneth.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/testing-volume-by-tier); under US$10K/month, 8–12 distinct concepts a month — [Common Thread Collective](https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/meta-andromeda-roas-creative-strategy-2026)
- Catalog ads and enriched DPA figures — [Common Thread Collective](https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/meta-andromeda-roas-creative-strategy-2026), [QuickAds](https://www.quickads.ai/d2c-fashion-creative-intelligence)
- TikTok image/carousel guidance and results — [TikTok Image Ads Playbook](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/Image_Ads_Carousel_Ads_Playbook.pdf)

### Inferences

**Ranked shortlist (researcher judgement: evidence strength for apparel × buildable from flat-lay stills without touching the garment × fit to a small, premium, identity-driven fight brand)**

| Rank | Format | Why it ranks here | Buildable from real stills + code/AI bg/VO |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | S10 Bold-claim headline + product hero, with S1 feature-callout as its sibling | Top-2 asset type; most apparel swipe-file examples; flat-lay is the ideal input; 350gsm is a ready-made claim | Yes |
| 2 | S5 Post-it / handwritten note on product photo | #1 hit rate in apparel; near-zero production cost; structurally different from #1 | Yes |
| 3 | S18 Restock / drop announcement (static) + V14 countdown teaser | Newness and urgency are the top hook tactics; fits fight-calendar drops and a small catalogue | Yes |
| 4 | S8 Social comment / comment-reply | #7 hit rate in apparel; handles the "is it actually heavy / does the print last" objection with real comments | Yes (needs real comments) |
| 5 | S14 Real cutout on AI-generated scene (stylized product shot) | #3 hit rate in apparel; the one place generative imagery adds clear value without touching the garment | Yes, with strict layering |
| 6 | S12 Offer-first / bundle tiers (hoodie + jersey + shorts) with S19 price anchor | Highest-volume winning format overall and top spend-use hooks; use sparingly to protect premium positioning | Yes |
| 7 | V10 Slideshow / photo carousel (TikTok and Reels) | Best-evidenced no-talent video; apparel spend-use #7; TikTok's own data | Yes |
| 8 | S22 Enriched catalog / DPA overlay | Strong agency data for catalog ads; automates retargeting for a small team | Yes |
| 9 | S7 Text-message thread (static) / V5 screen-record | #4 spend use in apparel; a third distinct "native" skin | Yes |

Lower priority for this brand: S2 us-vs-them and S15 reasons-why (evidence mainly from other categories), S6/S9 Notes and other UI skins (no apparel evidence; same concept as S7/S8), S4 press (only if real coverage exists), V1 kinetic explainer and V13 product spin (Animation under-performs; spin cannot be made fidelity-safe), V11 unboxing, V15 UGC mashup and V17 podcast (need real footage or people).

**Fidelity guard-rails that apply to every recipe (researcher synthesis)**

- The garment layer is always the original photograph: allowed operations are masking/cut-out, crop, scale, in-plane rotation, and global colour-space conversion. Not allowed: generative fill touching the garment, AI upscaling, relighting, recolouring, try-on, depth-warp parallax, image-to-video on the product.
- Generate backgrounds and scenes separately, with no garment in the prompt or reference, and composite in code; add shadows as separate code-drawn layers.
- All review, comment, message and press content must be real and verbatim; native skins must not fabricate third-party endorsement.
- Count concepts, not files: aim for 8–12 structurally different creatives a month drawn from at least 5 different rows of the table, rather than many variants of rank 1.

### Gaps

- The ranking is a judgement built on spend-based hit-rate ranks without values, live-ad observation and practitioner opinion; no source validates it for fightwear or for the Australian market.
- Whether the client has real reviews, customer comments, on-model measurements, per-colourway photos, or press — which gate ranks 4, 10, 13 and S4/S20 — is unknown.
- Relative performance of AI-scene composites versus plain flat-lay on a flat colour for apparel is untested in any source found.
