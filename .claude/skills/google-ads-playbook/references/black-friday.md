# Black Friday / Cyber Monday / Q4

## Month-by-month (Define Digital Academy)
- **August–September:** prioritise **new customer acquisition** (Shopping, search, Demand Gen, PMax). Anyone acquired now is more likely to repeat-buy at BFCM. September is analytical: find which creatives work and plan the creative for the next two months. Don't test the BF offer early; promoting BF deals in October flopped.
- **October:** scale **non-brand / generic** campaigns. Generic search volume climbs from late October, then collapses in BF week while **brand searches spike** (people research first). Accept about **20% below normal ROAS** while building the audience; judge top-of-funnel by engagement (time on site, pages per session, add to carts, signups). YouTube value/education content works now; save offer-heavy Shorts for the sale.
- **November:** start promoting the offer at most **2–3 weeks** before BF; early November is for building remarketing lists. Week 3: Demand Gen with creators/UGC; week 4: brand creators.
- **Promo PMax:** a separate PMax for the sale on **Maximize conversion value** or a very low tROAS, to scoop up remarketing demand, then switch it off afterwards.
- **Meta usually beats Google in sale periods**; put more of the incremental sale budget there. A new Demand Gen campaign is too slow to rely on for BF.
- Analogy: Aug–Sep = marketing fills the pipeline; Oct–early Nov = qualification; BFCM = sales' "hot season".

## Prep, Execute, Cooldown (Andrew Lolk)

### Prep (finish at least a week before)
1. **Analyse last year**: November–December day by day with change history. Where were you too aggressive or too timid? Compare ROAS by click date vs ROAS by conversion time to see how much pre-sale spend converted later.
2. Pre-sale growth predicts BF: if the month before BF is +30% YoY, BF will usually be about +30% too. Traffic going into November should already be at your growth target.
3. Build a **seasonality bid adjustment (SBA) plan per day** from last year (e.g. Mon 0%, Tue 20%, Wed 20%, Thu 40%).
4. If you use **max CPC caps** in portfolio strategies, keep the cap ≥ **3–4× normal CPC**, or SBAs do nothing.
5. **Raise budgets early** (budget-capped campaigns enter fewer auctions); control spend with tROAS.
6. Monthly-invoiced accounts: check the **credit limit** (unpaid balance counts) and request an increase; pay fortnightly if close.
7. Pre-write BF ad copy, promotion assets and countdowns, **and the Christmas copy**, so Cyber Monday flows straight into Christmas.
8. **No structural changes after Oct 1** (Nov 1 at the absolute latest). No big optimisations during BF week.

### Execute
- Bid on **blended ROAS / MER vs the daily target**; Google Ads ROAS lags too much intraday.
- Also compare **clicks and CPC by hour against the same weekday last year**. If CPC rises but clicks don't, there's no more volume and you're just inflating CPCs.
- Bidding is a **series of small bets**: adjust 1–3× a day (2–4× on Black Friday itself).
- Typical SBAs: 0% to 25–50% from Friday to Wednesday; be more aggressive on Thursday than feels comfortable.
- **Black Friday itself is often overspent**: much of Friday's revenue comes from earlier days' spend. 2026 plan: start BF at about +25% and build to about +50–70% through the day.
- **Saturday:** start defensive with no SBA, check around 12–1pm. **Sunday/Monday:** Cyber Monday is huge in the US, normal in Europe.
- A **Black Month** (month-long sale) lowers BF week performance and reduces the need for big SBAs.

### Cooldown
Apply **negative SBAs** after the sale (e.g. −30%, then −20% for a few days, then back to 0, often faster than you think) so you don't spend back the profit. Keep checking pacing and blended ROAS daily.

## New-account reality
A brand-new Google account a week or two before BF won't have the learning or data for any of this. Build brand search + bestseller Shopping, treat BF as a learning period, and position Google for December onwards.
